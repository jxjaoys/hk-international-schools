How to choose

Four things worth understanding before you shortlist — the pieces parents most often wish they'd known earlier.

The curriculum decides the destination

  • IB Diploma — broad, six-subject programme; the default in Hong Kong, and best if university destination is undecided. Scored /45.
  • A-Level — early specialisation in 3–4 subjects; a strong fit for UK (and increasingly global) universities.
  • AP — US-style transcript of 20+ advanced courses; the natural route to American universities.
  • HSC / Abitur — national pathways to Australia and Germany/Switzerland respectively.

Tuition is only the first number

Add a one-off debenture, capital levy, or nomination-rights fee (HK$250k–1.5m+ at private flagships; ESF asks ~HK$38k once per family), plus application fees, board-exam fees, and any EAL/learning support. Ask for a full schedule and total it across the whole school career, not one year.

Admissions run on a rolling clock

Applications usually open the autumn before the year of entry, and popular schools hold waitlists. Priority typically goes to siblings, staff children, debenture holders, and certain nationalities. Most include an assessment (written + interview, often in English and — for bilingual schools — Mandarin).

Read the numbers carefully

An IB average of 39 in a 30-student cohort is not the same as 37 in a 130-student cohort. Small, selective schools concentrate high averages; large ESF schools include a wider ability range. Weigh cohort size, selectivity, and fit alongside raw scores — and check what a school publishes about value-added, not just peaks.
